About

A Computer Scientist working in data, building toward markets.

I studied Computer Science at the University of the Witwatersrand, where I got hooked on the idea that complex systems, whether software, data or eventually markets, can be understood by breaking them down and modelling them. That instinct pulled me from writing programs toward working with data.

Today I'm a Data Analyst at iqbusiness, working with Python, SQL and business intelligence tools to turn messy data into things people can actually decide on. Alongside the day job, I read economics and follow financial markets closely, and I'm drawn to the parts where mathematics, probability and systematic decision-making meet uncertainty.

That's the direction I'm deliberately building toward: quantitative finance. Not as a title I'm claiming today, but as the place all of this (computer science, data, statistics and markets) is pointing.

Projects built in the open, where code and data answer real questions. Every link goes to a live repository.

01

V8: Quantitative Football Model in development

A production-style match-prediction and staking engine. Goal outcomes are modelled with Dixon-Coles (bivariate Poisson) using Bayesian shrinkage and time decay; probabilities are calibrated (isotonic / Platt, reliability diagrams, Brier / log-loss); bets are sized with fractional Kelly; and any edge is proven honestly through walk-forward backtesting and closing-line value. Every component maps to a transferable quant / risk technique.

PythonDixon-ColesCalibrationKelly stakingBacktesting

02

Credit Default Scorecard in development

An interpretable probability-of-default scorecard built the way a bank builds one: WOE / IV feature engineering, a logistic PD model, full validation (KS, Gini / AUC, PSI stability), PDO score scaling with reason codes, and a gradient-boosted benchmark to quantify the interpretability trade-off. Runs on the German Credit dataset and deliberately avoids leakage-prone sources.

PythonCredit riskWOE / IVLogistic PDValidation
